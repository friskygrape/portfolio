Overview

My TELUS is the primary self-service app for millions of Canadians to manage their wireless and home services. Despite strong install numbers, 30-day retention sat at 40.6% — most customers opened the app once and never returned.

I redesigned the onboarding and first-run experience to drive habitual engagement, increasing 30-day retention from 40.6% to 62.6% — a 22-point lift — within the first three months post-launch.

The Challenge

Usage data told a clear story: customers opened the app when something went wrong — a billing question, a service outage — then left. There was no pull to return. The app's home screen presented a static summary of account details that gave customers no reason to come back unless they had a specific task.

A deeper problem surfaced in diary studies: customers didn't know what the app could do for them. They associated My TELUS exclusively with bill payment, missing the self-service capabilities that would have saved them a trip to a store or a 20-minute support call.

The Approach

I started with a cohort analysis of retained versus churned users. The single strongest predictor of 30-day retention was completing at least two distinct self-service actions in the first session — customers who did this retained at 3× the baseline rate. The redesign focused on making that second action easy to discover.

I redesigned the onboarding flow to front-load the app's highest-value capabilities, contextualised to the customer's actual account. Rather than a generic feature tour, each customer saw prompts tied to their plan — a nudge to set up autopay for a wireless subscriber, a recommendation to check usage trends for a heavy data user.

The home screen was rebuilt around a personalised action card system — surfacing timely, relevant tasks (upcoming bill, data usage milestone, plan upgrade eligibility) instead of static account information. I worked closely with data science to define the ranking logic, ensuring cards were genuinely useful rather than manufactured engagement.

30-day retention climbed from 40.6% to 62.6% within the first quarter post-launch. Customers who completed the redesigned onboarding averaged 2.1 self-service actions per session, up from 0.9. The reduction in support call volume tied directly to the retention improvement, delivering an estimated $3.1M in annual cost savings.
